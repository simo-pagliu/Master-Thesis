\section{Results of the analysis}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Full Elicitation Sessions}
For this analysis, a total of four experts were consulted to perform the "full elicitation" process. The task included all the steps described in the previous section, here reported in short: (i) value functions of quantitative indicators, (ii) qualitative indicator values and their value functions, and (iii) weights by means of \gls{pilebwt}. The experts, who will remain anonymous, are presented in Table~\ref{tab:experts} along with their area of expertise.

\begin{table}[htbp]
	\centering
	\begin{tabular}{|c|c|}
		\hline
		\textbf{Expert} & \textbf{Area of Expertise} \\
		\hline
		E1 & Risk Assessment (Academia)\\
		E2 & Nuclear Safety (Academia) \\
		E4 & Nuclear Safety (Industry) \\
		E8 & Nuclear Economics (Academia)\\
		\hline
	\end{tabular}
	\caption{Experts involved in the elicitation process.}
	\label{tab:experts}
\end{table}

The first expert (E1) was involved in the first elicitation session that defined the starting point of value functions for the following experts (steps i and ii). On the other hand, expert E1 was also the least consistent in their \gls{pilebwt} (step iii), as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}, results will be therefore presented with and without their input.

The results of the weight elicitation sessions of the most and least consistent experts are presented in Figure~\ref{fig:weights_results}, as well as some notable examples of differently elicited value functions in Figure~\ref{fig:vf_examples}. The plots of the weight results represent the feasible weight space, obtained by exploration of the constraint space by means of a differential evolution algorithm (scipy library \cite{PLACEHOLDER}), as well as a comparison between the declared ratios from the pairwise comparisons and the obtained weights ratio from the optimization model. This plot was presented by \cite{PLACEHOLDER} to analyze the consistency of the results. Here, it is also presented with error bars to account for the weight space obtained: weights are sampled from the feasible weight space, the average is represented by the bars, and the standard deviation by the error bars.

\subsection{UP-MAVT}
The difference between the \gls{smc} and \gls{nsmc} was explained in Section~\ref{sec:up_mavt}. The results from the \gls{smc} are used to evaluate the overall uncertainty before aggregation and to judge the consensus among experts, while the \gls{nsmc} is used to obtain a final ranking. From the strict run, it is observed that there is significant consensus on the alternative values even if the weights might be different. Output for both methods for one of the countries is presented in Figure~\ref{fig:upmavt_results}.
