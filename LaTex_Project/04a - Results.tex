\section{Results of the analysis}
\label{sec:results}
Results from full elicitation sessions and abbreviated quick elicitation protocol are presented along with the experts that took part in the analysis. Finally, the output of the \gls{upmavt} framework is presented and the optimal technology alternatives for each national context are identified.

\subsection{Full Elicitation Sessions}
The elicitation process involved four experts who performed all analytical steps: (1) quantitative indicator value function development, (2) qualitative indicator assessment and value function construction, and (3) weight determination using the \gls{pilebwt} methodology. Expert backgrounds are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:experts}, identities are maintained confidential.

		\textbf{Expert} & \textbf{Area of Expertise} & \textbf{Experience}\\
		\hline
		E1 & Risk Assessment (Academia) & Senior Scientist\\
		E2 & Nuclear Safety (Academia) & Senior Scientist\\
		E4 & Nuclear Safety (Industry) & Junior Engineer\\
		E8 & Energy System Modelling (Academia) & PhD Student\\
		\hline
	\end{tabularx}
	\caption{Experts involved in the elicitation process.}
	\label{tab:experts}
\end{table}

Expert E1 participated in the "first elicitation" that established baseline value functions for subsequent evaluations (steps 1 and 2). However, as detailed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}, this expert demonstrated the lowest consistency in weight elicitation (step 3). The analysis therefore presents results both including and excluding E1's contributions to assess the ranking sensitivity to this outlier perspective.

Figure~\ref{fig:weight_space_results} shows weight elicitation outcomes presenting the feasible weight space, explored via differential evolution (SciPy implementation \cite{PLACEHOLDER}), Figure~\ref{fig:consistency_results} presents the consistency plots, which compare the Declared pairwise comparison ratios with the ratios between the optimized weights. Each second bar represents the mean weight sampled from the feasible solution space, with error bars indicating $\pm \sigma$ to quantify solution variability. This adapted presentation, building on \cite{PLACEHOLDER}'s consistency analysis, explicitly incorporates uncertainty bounds from the weight space exploration. Figure~\ref{fig:vf_examples} presents the value functions elicited from the four experts for the same indicator, showing the divergencies between expert opinions.

\subsection{UP-MAVT}
As established in Section~\ref{sec:uncertanty_propagation_mcda}, the \gls{smc} and \gls{nsmc} approaches serve complementary purposes. \gls{smc} results evaluate pre-aggregation uncertainty and expert consensus levels, while \gls{nsmc} generates final rankings. The analysis reveals notable consensus on alternative values despite weight variations. Representative outputs for one of the countries are presented in Figure~\ref{fig:upmavt_results}, comparing both methodological approaches.
