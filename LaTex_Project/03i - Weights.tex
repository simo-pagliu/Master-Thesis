\section{Weights}
\label{sec:weights}
The last part of the elicitation is the definition of the weights applying the \gls{bwt} methodology presented in section \ref{sec:bwt}. This session was deemed intensive from the experts due to the intrinsic difficulty of the questions at hand. An observation that must not be interpreted as negative, since it properly reflects the difficulty of a tradeoff problem. Nonetheless, this has to be considered by the analyst when planning elicitation sessions, by properly allocating time and breaks, to avoid tiredness and loss of focus that could compromise the quality of the answers. For the comparison another graphical tool was developed in python, screenshots of the interface are presented in Figure \ref{fig:weights_elicitation}. Unfortunately in this case it is not possible to circumvent the time-consuming nature of this task due to the method itself requiring the expert to reason about all tradeoffs. To ensure consistency, since the \gls{bwt} method requires the knowledge of value functions, only experts that already participated in the value function elicitation sessions were involved in this step. For consistency checking, an "on-the-fly" method was implemented in the graphical tool. The software keeps track of the ordinal consistency of the answers and provides a clue to the analyst by indicating the points in the data range that do or do not respect ordinal consistency. This is done by changing the colour of the slider across the values and by adding arrows to better identify the boundary points. This allows the analyst to easily spot inconsistencies without interfering with the process. The analysis might deem the inconsistency to be minor and therefore acceptable, or might decide to ask the expert for a refinement or a revision of his/her judgment.
