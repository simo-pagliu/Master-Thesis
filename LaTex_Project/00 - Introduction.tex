The global energy landscape is undergoing a significant transformation, driven by the imperative to decarbonize electricity generation while ensuring energy security and system reliability. Within this context, the European energy transition is accelerating and nuclear power can play a critical role to both provide low carbon electricity and stabilize the grid amid the increase use of renewable power sources. 

National requirements in terms of energy vary significantly, while some prioritize baseload capacity to offset import dependence, others require flexible solutions to be coupled with existing and future renewables. The emergence of \glspl{smr} alongside established Generation III+ designs expands possibility but also introduces complexities to the decision making process. The committent to a specific nuclear reactor design involves complex trade-offs across  across technical, economic, regulatory, social, environmental, and geopolitical dimensions, which economic, social and political consequences will last for decades.

This thesis deveolops a method based on an established decision support methodology, \gls{mavt}, to deal with the uncertanties arising from the \gls{mcda} process. Graphical digital tools are developed to facilitate expert elicitation sessions and result presentation as well as procedures to charaterize the uncertanty of qualiative and subjective information.

The framework is then applied in chapter 3 (\ref{sec:case_study}) to systematically evaluate and rank nuclear reactor designs for potential deployment in different European countries and examines how different national contexts may influence the assessment process. 

The research addresses the following key questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item How to address the uncertanty of the qualitative and subjective elements intrinsic to the \gls{mcda} process?
    \item How to systematically evaluate and present uncertain results in a \gls{mavt} framework?
    \item Which reactor designs are viable for European deployment based on geopolitical issues, technology maturity, and market availability?
    \item What are the key technical, economic and social attributes that differentiate the viable reactor designs?
    \item How do the energy requirements and socio-political contexts of different European countries influence these attributes?
    \item How does the application of a \gls{mavt} framework reveal differences in reactor design rankings across countries?
\end{itemize}
