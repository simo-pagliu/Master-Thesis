\section{Suggestions for further improvements and extensions}
\label{sec:suggestions}
In this section, suggestions for future improvements and extensions to the work of this thesis are provided. These suggestions are divided into three categories: improvements on the case study, improvements on the elicitation process, and improvements on the \gls{upmavt} method.

\subsection{Improvements for future case studies}
This analysis adopted a conservative approach by relying on current data for key indicators. Future work could enhance predictive value by incorporating forecasted trajectories for emissions, costs, electricity prices, and demand, as well as quantitative risk assessments, and market projections. Such improvements would better capture the dynamic nature of broad energy systems.

For potential siting integration, the following indicators are recommended:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Landscape impact (social perspective)
    \item Grid integration requirements (technical)
    \item Demand proximity (technical/economic)
    \item Local employment potential (social)
    \item National economic development impact (economic)
    \item Industrial proximity for construction logistics
    \item Water system impacts (agency assessment perspective)
    \item Land use changes (pre-construction baseline)
    \item Ancillary infrastructure requirements (qualitative assessment)
\end{itemize}

Biodiversity impacts are intentionally excluded, assuming preliminary site screening would eliminate protected areas and high-value ecosystems from consideration. All environmental indicators should reflect regulatory agency perspectives rather than public perception metrics.

At the national level, incorporating an import reduction objective could provide valuable strategic insights. This would enable direct comparison of deployment configurations, such as evaluating a single large reactor against several \glspl{smr} delivering equivalent capacity, while maintaining focus on energy security outcomes.

\subsection{Improvements on the Elicitaiton process}
The most significant potential improvement to the elicitation process would be developing interactive software to enable remote, self-paced expert participation, reduced analyst supervision requirements and expanded expert panel capacity

Such a system would simultaneously decrease participant burden and enhance result robustness through broader specialist engagement.

\subsubsection{Value function elicitation}
The sequential elicitation approach, beginning with a comprehensive "first elicitation" followed by targeted corrections by other experts, proved highly effective in accelerating the process, as anticipated.

During value function elicitation, the rigid adherence to pre-defined indifference points from initial mid-splitting created unnecessary constraints. Future implementations should permit greater expert flexibility in adjusting these reference points during subsequent sessions.

\subsubsection{\gls{qi} approaches}
Regarding \glspl{qi} the use of a standardized reference alternative significantly improved consistency and is recommended for future studies. On the other hand, the "spectrum" approach for the "construction complexity" indicator (Section~\ref{sec:economic_indicators}) caused substantial expert confusion and should be replaced with conventional ranking methods.

Building on the expert interaction patterns observed in Section~\ref{sec:expert_observations}, future studies should consider implementing knowledge-sharing sessions prior to individual elicitations. Such pre-elicitation exchanges would allow experts to present case-specific insights, potentially reducing deviations that stem from information asymmetries rather than genuine differences in professional judgment. This approach maintains individual assessment integrity while ensuring all experts operate from a common factual baseline.

\subsubsection{\gls{pilebwt} method observations}
The digital interface significantly facilitated the elicitation process, though expert guidance by the analyist remained essential for proper method application. The primary challenge was the inherent difficulty of quantifying pairwise comparisons, a task demanding substantial cognitive effort that should be accounted for in session planning.

A critical observation concerned methodological adherence: experts frequently defaulted to intuitive importance comparisons ("How much more important is A than B?") rather than the required compensatory trade-off formulation ("How much must A improve from its minimum to offset B's decline from minimum to maximum?"). Future applications must emphasize this distinction during expert briefings to maintain methodological rigor.

\subsection{Methodological Enhancements for UP-MAVT}
The most valuable enanchement to \gls{upmavt} would be the implementation of uncertainty tracking. This would enable targeted uncertainty mitigation and more transparent results. The tracking framework should aim to document uncertainty origins, trace its propagation pathways and quantify contribution magnitudes.

Development of formal consistency-based weighting mechanisms for expert inputs could substantially improve result reliability, particularly in multi-stakeholder contexts where specialist perspectives vary in coherence.

Finally, the author suggests exploring the extension of uncertainty propagation techniques to other \gls{mcda} frameworks
